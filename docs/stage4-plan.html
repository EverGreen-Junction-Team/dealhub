Stage 4 · Written-channel AI extraction Clarity & alignment

Turning raw conversations into structured deal intelligence.

A staged plan that first extracts the facts hiding inside conversations (MEDDIC, buying signals, deal shape) then turns silence and rep behaviour into actionable manager alerts — with human-checkable evidence, versioned prompts, and safe writeback to GHL.

Where we are today

Stage 1 through Stage 3 are live in production across all four sub-accounts. The database captures every contact, opportunity, message, task, and note, reconciles daily against GHL, and exposes clean analytics through vw_opportunity_flat and vw_opportunity_messages_json. Attribution between messages and deals is versioned via message_opportunity_map, with 5 confidence-rated rules and supersede-only history.

What that gives us for Stage 4: every message is stored, cleaned, time-anchored, attributed to a deal, and traceable back to the exact GHL webhook that produced it. Stage 4 sits on top of this — we don't need to fetch or clean anything, we just need to read and interpret.

What Stage 4 delivers

Today, MEDDIC fields on an opportunity are only filled in when a rep manually types them. Most deals sit half-filled, or filled once and never updated. The conversations themselves — SMS, email, webchat, notes — contain the answers, but nobody has time to re-read them.

Stage 4 builds an extraction layer that reads the approved conversation for each deal and produces:

  • Structured MEDDIC answers — one AI-derived value per field, per opportunity.
  • Evidence receipts — the exact verbatim quote from the source message, with sender, date, and channel.
  • Confidence scores — so low-signal answers are flagged for review, not silently displayed.
  • Version history — every extraction is an append-only snapshot; prior answers are never overwritten silently.
  • Optional writeback to GHL — approved values project to opportunity.meddic_1_ai through meddic_10_ai, so reps see them in GHL directly.

Every published fact is backed by a quote. If the AI can't find evidence, the field stays empty rather than guessing.

What the AI can tell us

There are two distinct jobs an AI layer can do on top of this data, and they matter to different people. Naming them separately keeps the scope honest.

Job A

What does the deal say about itself?

Read the conversation, produce structured facts. MEDDIC, Three Whys, buying signals. Improves reporting and gives reps a head-start on qualification. This is what the CSV spec describes.

Job B

What does the absence of activity tell us?

Detect stalled deals, unanswered customer questions, missed follow-ups, stale qualification, ghosting patterns. Arguably more valuable because it's actionable — it tells a manager who needs to do what, right now.

Job A improves reporting. Job B changes rep behaviour. Both feed off the same underlying data — messages, notes, tasks, stage history, custom fields — but the questions they answer are different, and so is what they surface.

The seven categories in detail

Everything AI can meaningfully extract from the data we have today, grouped by what it answers.

01Deal facts

What the deal is

Extracted from message content · MEDDIC / Three Whys territory
  • What the customer wants — product, size, materials, style, quantity
  • Why they want it — replacement, renovation, new build, aesthetic
  • When they need it — deadline mentions, "before Christmas", "next month"
  • Budget signals — explicit numbers, ranges, price sensitivity language
  • Who's involved — partner, architect, builder, project manager
  • Decision process — "I need to check with my wife", "the architect will decide"
  • Site details — postcode, property type, planning permission, listed status
  • Competitor mentions — other companies contacted, quoted, seen
  • Blockers — planning, funding, timing, existing installer
02Temperature

How hot is this deal?

Tone and pattern of communication, not just facts
  • Urgency signals — "need this ASAP", "we've been waiting weeks"
  • Enthusiasm — long detailed replies, follow-up questions from customer
  • Cooling — one-word replies, days between responses, no follow-up
  • Frustration — repeat questions, "as I said before", complaint language
  • Buying signals — "how do we proceed", "when can we book"
  • Recurring objections — same concern raised across multiple messages
  • Silence pattern — customer went dark after being engaged
03Rep behaviour

What is the rep doing — or not doing?

Discipline signals · The core of Job B
  • Days since last outbound — is the rep chasing?
  • Response latency — how long the rep took to reply to inbound
  • Unanswered customer questions — customer asked X, rep never addressed it
  • Missed commitments — rep said "I'll send the quote by Friday" and Friday's passed
  • No qualification captured — Three Whys empty despite clear signals in the conversation
  • Vague replies — rep's language quality flagged where it lacks specifics
  • Task follow-through — task marked complete but no evidence in messages
  • Skipped stage steps — deal at "Quote Sent" but no quote was actually sent
  • Stale MEDDIC — filled 6 weeks ago, customer's situation has moved since
04Attribution

Where did this deal come from and how has it evolved?

Journey and channel patterns
  • First-touch channel — website / phone / referral
  • Journey pattern — how many messages before serious engagement
  • Conversion moments — which message shifted them from cold to warm
  • Loss moments — which message coincided with them going dark
  • Cross-channel — SMS then email then phone, or all in one channel?
  • Human vs automation — time-to-first-response we already track
05Risk & forecast

What is this deal likely to do next?

Predictive signals about outcome
  • Stall risk — pattern-matched against how past lost deals looked
  • Ghosting risk — customer inactivity that historically preceded losses
  • Bad-fit signals — postcode outside service area, budget below range, timeline unrealistic
  • Great-fit signals — budget confirmed, timeline set, decision-maker confirmed
  • Win probability — derived score from all above
  • Expected close date — inferred from customer's stated timeline
  • Forecast drift — rep's forecast vs AI-derived timeline
06Cross-deal

What patterns emerge across many deals?

Per-rep, per-product, per-source aggregation
  • Rep coaching — "this rep has 5 deals with unanswered pricing questions"
  • Product-mix trends — "Crittall Corten enquiries up 40% this quarter"
  • Source quality — "Facebook leads convert at 3× lower rate than Google"
  • Objection frequency — top objections this month
  • Winning language — reply patterns that correlate with wins
07Silence

When nothing has happened, what does that tell us?

Absence-based insights · Highest actionable value
  • Time since last rep action — X days silent
  • Open deals with zero rep-authored messages — never actually worked
  • Customer questions still unanswered — days after being asked
  • Stage moved without matching outbound — "Quote Sent" but no message exists
  • MEDDIC gaps despite rich conversation — 20 messages of signal, nothing captured
  • Automation-only replies — no human has actually engaged
  • Missed positive-signal follow-ups — customer said "yes, send me the quote" 3 days ago

Why this matters for scope

A common mistake with AI extraction projects is to build category 01 in isolation and call it done. That gives you a reporting improvement but not a behavioural one. Category 03, 05 and 07 — rep behaviour, risk signals, and silence detection — are what actually change how the sales team operates day to day.

The build path below phases these deliberately: Job A first (categories 01 – 02) because it's the foundation, Job B second (03, 05, 07) because it depends on the extraction outputs from Job A, then the cross-deal analytics (06) on top.

How the pipeline works

Each extraction is a controlled operation with a full audit trail — from the input messages to the final answer stored in Supabase and (optionally) written back to GHL.

  1. The extraction engine reads the deal's approved conversation from vw_opportunity_messages_json.
  2. An LLM (model and prompt version pinned per run) is called with that conversation and a strict output schema.
  3. Every returned fact must include a verbatim quote from the source. Facts without evidence are dropped.
  4. Results write to three tables: extraction_runs (the run itself), opportunity_extractions (this deal at this point in time), extracted_facts + fact_evidence (the answers and their receipts).
  5. If writeback is enabled, approved values project to opportunity.meddic_N_ai in GHL, logged in ghl_writebacks for idempotency.

A rep or admin can review any answer, see the quote that produced it, agree or override. Overrides create a new version — the AI's original answer is never lost.

Build path

Phased deliberately so Job A lands before Job B, and each phase produces something useful on its own. Each phase boundary is a natural checkpoint to review progress and decide whether to proceed.

0

Foundations

Prompt design Evaluation set Field spec locked

Draft the extraction prompt for MEDDIC + Three Whys + signals, agree on the exact output schema, and build a small evaluation set of ~30 hand-picked deals across the four sub-accounts — deals where the correct answers are known. This is where we lock in what "good" looks like before we spend any model time.

1

Job A · Extraction engine

Categories 01 – 02 Reads conversations Produces facts

Ship the tables, wire the LLM call, run extraction manually on the evaluation set, compare AI answers to ground truth, iterate on the prompt until quality clears the bar set in Phase 0. Every fact is evidence-backed. Facts without evidence are dropped.

2

Manual input & refresh

Web app hook Instant re-extract

Sales manager pastes new information into the web app — an email sent outside the system, a call note, a transcript — clicks a button and the MEDDIC fields refresh immediately. Extraction fires on the deal, the new source is included, GHL is mirrored back for rep visibility. No waiting for the nightly sweep. The manager sees an updated answer in seconds.

3

Scheduled runs & cost controls

Nightly extraction Staleness detection

Nightly extraction runs across all open deals whose sources have changed since the last successful run. Staleness is tracked per-deal as two watermarks so the web app can show Current, New info since last read, or Never extracted with no calculation. Hard cost caps prevent accidental overrun.

4

Review & GHL writeback

Human-in-the-loop Idempotent projection

Low-confidence answers stay unapproved until a human confirms. Approved values project to GHL — rep sees AI-derived insight on the opportunity in their existing GHL view, alongside their own values, neither overwriting the other. Every writeback is logged with the source fact, provider response, and retry state.

5

Job B · Rep behaviour & silence signals

Categories 03 · 05 · 07 Actionable alerts

Second AI pass on top of the Job A outputs plus what we already track in the analytics view (whys freshness, task discipline, response latency, message counts). Surfaces stalled deals, unanswered customer questions, missed follow-ups, ghosting patterns, stale qualification. Delivered as a manager-facing dashboard — one screen that shows the deals that need attention right now, ranked by risk.

6

Cross-deal analytics & coaching signals

Category 06 Aggregate insight

Patterns across many deals — per-rep coaching signals, product-mix trends, source quality comparisons, top objections this month, winning language. This is where the coaching plans and battle cards from the CSV blueprint's later stages live.

Realistic time expectations

Honest read on timeline

Stage 4 is not a quick build. It's an evolving system that gets more accurate as we tune the prompt against real data. The first working version lands relatively quickly; the version that reliably matches what a good rep would write takes sustained iteration on top of that.

This is normal for LLM systems. What matters is that every intermediate version is safely reviewable — no silent overwrites, no unapproved writeback, no hallucinated facts. We move quickly on infrastructure and slowly on quality until the client is comfortable.

1 Job A extraction engine + evaluation loop Medium · the core build
2 Manual input & refresh flow Small · web app hook
3 Scheduled runs + cost controls + staleness UI Small

Timelines are deliberately not fixed dates. This kind of build moves at the pace of prompt iteration — the code parts are predictable, the quality parts are not. What each phase delivers is a working baseline; accuracy improves with iteration against real data and there is no honest way to put a date on that.

A rough shape: Job A (Phases 0 – 4) is the first major milestone — once it lands, the client sees the AI producing MEDDIC values with evidence, and the manager can refresh a deal from the web app. Job B (Phase 5) is the second major milestone, dependent on Job A being stable. Cross-deal analytics (Phase 6) sits on top of everything and is worth building last, when there's enough data on both jobs to make aggregation meaningful.

What we need to lock down

  1. MEDDIC field definitions in the client's own words. What does "Metrics" mean for a Crittall deal specifically? What does "Economic buyer" look like for a Bespoke Glazing Design customer? These become the ground truth for the prompt.
  2. Ten to thirty labelled deals as an evaluation set. Deals where the correct MEDDIC values have been written manually by a strong rep or the sales lead. This is the answer key the AI's outputs are measured against.
  3. Writeback rules. When AI confidence is 0.9+, write automatically? Always require human approval? Different rules per field? These are policy decisions, not technical ones.
  4. Which sub-account goes live first. Recommend the sub-account with the fullest dataset and clearest read on ground truth. That's usually the flagship client — for us, Crittall.

What we own on our side

  • LLM provider selection, key management, rate-limit and retry handling.
  • Prompt versioning and A/B evaluation infrastructure.
  • All 5 new tables + 21 new columns on opportunities.
  • Cost tracking per run, per location, per model version.
  • Complete audit trail — every AI answer traceable to model, prompt, run, and source quotes.
  • Safe GHL writeback (idempotent, retryable, never overwrites rep-authored fields).
  • Regular quality reports through the early iteration window post-launch.
